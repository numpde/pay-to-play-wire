# Invoice design

## Status and purpose

The five-field design was agreed in the Mail workshop and confirmed by the user's JSON example on 2026-10-22. It supersedes the eleven- and twelve-field proposals and was released as 0.2.0. The payload field decisions and consumer ownership below remain applicable. The later JWS direction in notes/008_jws_profile.txt supersedes this note's domain-prefix signing/hash construction and envelope APIs for maintained source 0.3.0. Published 0.1.x and 0.2.0 artifacts retain their original grammar and exact bytes. Qualification and release status for 0.2.0 are recorded in notes/007_five_field_implementation.txt; neither note establishes current consumer deployment.

An invoice is the provider's signed offer: the service promised to a beneficiary for an explicit payment amount and asset, with a best-effort validity time. A payment authorization references the hash of that exact offer. Provider acceptance, payment collection and service fulfillment remain separate decisions and effects.

## Approved shape

Exactly five top-level members are present. The notation below describes types, not final wire bytes or a complete JSON example.

```text
{
  provider: <encoded public key>,
  payment: {
    asset: <CAIP-19 asset identifier>,
    amount: <base-unit integer string>
  },
  bestBefore: <Unix seconds>,
  beneficiary: <encoded public key>,
  service: <freeform JSON value>
}
```

## User's JSON example

```json
{
  "provider": "<encoded public key>",
  "payment": {
    "asset": "eip155:1/slip44:60",
    "amount": "1000000000000000"
  },
  "bestBefore": 2000003600,
  "beneficiary": "<encoded public key>",
  "service": {
    "address": "agent@mail.dapp32.com"
  }
}
```

Every field is covered by the signature and exact-byte hash. Mail alone interprets service.address; the shared codec treats service as opaque JSON, and bestBefore remains a best-effort hint. The key placeholders do not settle the public-key encoding or numeric bounds.

## Field decisions

- `provider` binds the signing identity into the offer and its derived hash. It replaces merchant UUID and key-alias indirection. Verification must require the decoded provider key to equal the key that verifies the signature; separate trust checks on two different keys are insufficient. A document-supplied key does not establish its own trust: the application verifies registration and authorization for the intended provider.
- `payment.asset` states the offered unit explicitly using CAIP-19. The identifier includes chain and asset identity; it does not identify or qualify a payment contract, establish token behavior or guarantee that a consumer supports the asset.
- `payment.amount` commits to the price as an integer string in the asset's base units. No floating-point conversion, ticker interpretation or implicit denomination is introduced.
- `bestBefore` expresses best-effort offer validity in Unix seconds. Receipt before it does not guarantee acceptance; receipt after it permits the provider to drop the offer, without requiring every consumer to reject it. The codec validates its wire representation without consulting a clock or making an acceptance decision.
- `beneficiary` binds the intended recipient's public key, independently of the payer. The workshop retains this as an explicit shared field rather than hiding recipient authorization inside an opaque product schema. Possession and authority remain application checks.
- `service` commits to the provider's service description using any JSON value kind, including objects, arrays, strings, numbers, booleans or null, within the document's strict JSON and resource limits. The provider owns its meaning and validation. The shared codec preserves exact bytes and lossless numbers without imposing Mail's schema on other providers.

## Removed fields and their owners

- `version`: the caller's trusted signing context selects the grammar. Do not retain a document marker, infer a trusted profile from untrusted contents or try legacy profiles until a signature verifies.
- `merchantId` and `merchantKeyId`: the encoded provider public key supplies signing identity; application enrollment supplies trust and any account association. Do not restore a UUID or key alias inside the document.
- `invoiceId` and an embedded `invoiceHash`: identity remains outside the five-field payload. In 0.3.0 it hashes the complete signed compact JWS, including its signature; see notes/008_jws_profile.txt. Identical signed artifacts have the same identity. Issuance recovery, exclusive-offer policy and duplicate fulfillment are server responsibilities.
- `customer` and `channelId`: payment authorization and the accepted purchase supply payer and channel. They do not restrict the merchant offer. P2P's authoritative purchase ledger must serialize first acceptance by invoice hash across all payer/channel positions. A competing valid payment voucher can remain collectible after admission rejection; P2P must retain and account for that authorization. Mail's own store separately enforces single fulfillment.
- Separate `chainId`, `paymentContract` and `quotedPayee`: CAIP-19 supplies the offered chain and asset, while P2P qualifies the authorized settlement route and payee through provider registration and voucher context. Asset equality alone cannot qualify a route or protect against mutable payees. This responsibility is outside the shared document grammar.
- `acceptBy` and `redeemBy`: the design supplies only best-effort `bestBefore`. Do not reintroduce either hard cutoff under another name or treat `bestBefore` as an automatic reservation-release, collection or first-fulfillment deadline.
- `serviceTerms`: `service` replaces the required product-object shape and allows arbitrary provider-owned JSON values.

## Settled wire representation

- Both keys are strings containing canonical unpadded base64url of exactly 32 raw Ed25519 public bytes (43 characters). The trusted signing profile and Ed25519 key APIs fix the algorithm; there is no document algorithm negotiation. Reuse Mail's public-JWK x decoder rules and workshop string representation, without the JWK wrapper, aliases or prefixes. Reject alternate alphabets, padding, lengths and unused pad-bit spellings.
- Accept generic CAIP-19 asset_type and optional /token_id syntax, with CAIP-2 chain syntax and the standard component length bounds. Preserve case and spelling, including percent characters; impose no namespace-specific canonicalization or payment support policy. Maximum complete identifier length is 258 characters. A syntactically accepted identifier is not a qualified payable asset.
- Amounts match [1-9][0-9]* and stay strings. The scalar helper caps digits at the existing 16384-byte document resource bound; complete documents still must fit that byte limit. There is no conversion to a Python integer or EVM uint256 policy. Payment adapters own narrower bounds.
- bestBefore must be a canonical JSON integer token in 0..2^53-1. No negative zero, string, boolean, fractional or exponent spelling is accepted. Decode it to a Python integer only after bounded lexical validation, without consulting a clock.
- The released 0.2.0 reference consumer profile used P2P-INVOICE-4 followed by LF: hex 5032502d494e564f4943452d340a. That historical domain API and its invoice identity are superseded by the fixed JWS profile and full-artifact hash in notes/008_jws_profile.txt. There is no legacy verification path in maintained source.

CAIP-19 reference: https://standards.chainagnostic.org/CAIPs/caip-19

## Shared API cutover

The released 0.2.0 construction concatenated an explicit domain with exact original UTF-8 payload bytes, signed the preimage with Ed25519 and hashed that preimage. It remains an immutable historical release. Maintained 0.3.0 source instead uses RFC 7515 JWS with RFC 9864 Ed25519 and hashes the complete compact signed artifact; notes/008_jws_profile.txt is authoritative for that signing input, identity and API. Verification never reconstructs original protected or payload bytes.

Retain the existing document limits of 16384 encoded bytes and container depth 16, counting the root object as depth 1. Reject duplicate decoded member names at every depth, malformed UTF-8/JSON, BOMs, nonfinite numbers and unpaired Unicode surrogates. Supporting every service value kind does not remove these limits or permit rounding nested numbers.

The five-field verification API must bind the encoded provider to the actual signature-verification key and remove the merchant UUID and key-alias arguments. Caller-owned trust and authorization still apply; successful cryptographic verification alone does not enroll the provider or accept the offer.

Remove validate_payment_route and its public export: it reads chainId and paymentContract, which no longer exist. P2P owns selection and qualification of its authorized route and comparison with payment.asset. Do not keep an old-grammar path or introduce a route-comparison facade to preserve the former API. Update the package payload inventory, installed-artifact checks and API guide in this repository; consumer owners separately update their own calls and qualify adoption. The new scalar exports are require_public_key, require_asset, require_amount and require_best_before. Remove obsolete merchant, identifier, EVM and deadline exports.

Validation of the completed cutover must reject a signature by a different trusted provider than the key named in the document, reject legacy members, preserve arbitrary service values including null and lossless numeric scalars, and keep an otherwise well-formed bestBefore value independent of the clock. Installed-artifact qualification must exercise that same final grammar and API rather than retained old fixtures.

## Consumer lifecycle obligations

P2P is the accounting service. A payment voucher is the payer's signed payment authorization. A payment position is that payer's payment state on one channel in one chain/contract deployment. These concepts belong to payment admission and accounting; they are not additional invoice members.

Mail must reconcile exclusive address reservations, offer reuse, accepted-purchase recovery and fulfillment with the absence of a signed redemption deadline. Releasing an unaccepted reservation requires authoritative P2P admission state and coordination that prevents acceptance racing address release; an uncertain admission outcome cannot be treated as rejection. The coordination protocol remains to be designed. This note does not invent a replacement timer or field and does not claim that best-effort offer validity resolves those races.

P2P resolves the provider through its uniquely registered invoice-purpose public key and must qualify the provider-authorized route, requested asset, observed payee, settlement deployment and voucher-selected position through its own trusted context. Recording a channel reference neither grants the provider permission to use that payout route nor qualifies its payee or asset. The accepted purchase and retained evidence must bind the actual admitted route and position. A valid payment authorization can remain on-chain authority even when the offer is declined or the purchase loses first acceptance; rejection does not revoke that authorization or establish a refund.

P2P may initially decline offers received after `bestBefore`. Accepting a late offer requires assurance that the provider can still fulfill it. Mail cannot reclaim a reserved address from a timestamp alone when acceptance has happened or its outcome is uncertain. These are consumer lifecycle prerequisites, not reasons to restore invoice fields.

P2P records the actual policy, decision time and provider qualification for a fresh acceptance. Recovery of an already accepted purchase is a separate operation and must not repeat fresh admission or reject that accepted purchase merely because bestBefore has passed. Mail retains its own beneficiary authorization, single-fulfillment and receipt-recovery responsibilities. These consumer lifecycle prerequisites must be resolved before production payment promises; they do not block implementation of the shared codec.

The shared package continues to own strict JSON, exact-byte hashing/signing, JWS handling and installed interoperability vectors. Provider admission, enrollment, reservation release, collection, retention, refunds and fulfillment remain consumer-owned. Every format cutover requires fresh qualification of declared immutable package and consumer inputs; earlier candidate runs prove neither a later artifact nor deployment.
